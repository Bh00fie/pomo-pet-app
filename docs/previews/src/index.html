M0 is scaffolded, and 3D was put on trial

Two pieces of work. The Expo SDK 54 app now exists, type-checks and bundles. And the question the Forest app raised — should the aquarium be real 3D? — has a working prototype and an answer.

Recommendation · defer 3D to v2

3D is feasible — it runs in App Store Expo Go with no dev client, so the free build plan survives. It is deferred because a new fish species costs hours as a 2D parameter record and days as a modelled, rigged 3D asset — and selling species is the business model. Everything below is the evidence.

The spike · three tank shapes, running

Fish behaviour is authored once; each tank supplies a containment function

Real swim math and real fish geometry from src/features/aquarium3d/, ported to a 2D canvas so this page stays 25 KB instead of shipping three.js. What it shows faithfully is the shape language and the motion. What it cannot show is material quality or frame cost.

Fish At 20: 1,760 triangles, ~80 draw calls.

Why tank shapes are not the deciding factor

Adding a SKU means adding a record plus one containment function — a box clamps per axis, a cylinder clamps the horizontal radius, a bowl normalises onto a sphere. Because containment is the shape equation rather than a per-frame collision test, fish can never clip the glass. The identical pattern works in 2D with a Skia Path and a clip. 3D makes a round bowl a better purchase; it does not make it a possible one.

The expo-gl backend finding came from reading the shipped source: ios/EXGLContext.mm creates an EAGLContext and renders into a CAEAGLLayer. That is OpenGL ES, which Apple deprecated in 2018 — not Metal, and not ANGLE-on-Metal. Skia is on Metal. A slow-moving risk, but it points the wrong way.

Not measured: frame rate, battery, thermals. Nothing ran on a physical iPhone. Those figures in the full report are reasoned estimates and are labelled as such.
